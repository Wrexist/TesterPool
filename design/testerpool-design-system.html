Design System · v1.0 · August 2026

Get your 12.
Keep them 14 days.
Ship.

Google Play requires 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days before a personal developer account can publish to production. TesterPool puts ~15 indie Android developers into a Pod — everyone tests everyone else's app for the same 14 days, with buffer seats, daily engagement proof, and rescue testers when someone drops. Closed-track only. No store reviews, no ratings, no production installs — ever.

The tester network that won't get your app pulled.

How it works

Three steps. Fourteen days. One shot.

1
Join a pod of 15

Matched by category and device mix. You need 12 — we seat 15, so three people can disappear and you still clear the bar.

2
Test daily for 14 days

Everyone checks in with screenshot proof. Google rejects for low engagement as often as for missing testers — so engagement is what we measure.

Ship with evidence

Day 14 generates your Production Evidence Pack — engagement stats and a feedback digest you paste straight into Google's production-access form.

27 — Principles

Six design principles

Written for the person who has to decide what a screen does at 2am, without asking.

01
The 14-day clock is always visible

Day N of 14 appears on every authenticated screen — dashboard, tester list, feedback form, even the wallet. The clock is the product's only real deadline, and a user should never have to navigate to find out how much of it is left.

02
Never let a dropout be a surprise

A missed check-in surfaces within the hour with the remedy attached — buffer status, rescue button, cost in credits. The failure mode we exist to eliminate is finding out on day 14 that the count was short on day 6.

03
Evidence over promises

Every claim in the UI is backed by an artifact a human can inspect: a screenshot, a timestamp, a rubric score, a device string. "13 testers active" is a link to thirteen verified check-in logs, not a number we assert.

04
Private feedback, publicly safe behaviour

Nothing a tester writes can leave the closed track. No path in the product produces a store review, a rating, or a production install. Where a competitor would put a share button, we put a lock icon and say why.

05
Reciprocity has to feel fair in both directions

Creators approve payouts, so the UI must make it impossible to quietly punish honesty. Critical feedback pays the same as praise, arbitration is one tap away for the tester, and abusing the flag costs the creator reliability.

06
Celebrate the graduation, not the grind

Daily check-ins get a quiet +5 and a filled square. Greenlight gets the full-screen moment and the shareable card. Reserve emotional volume for the outcome the developer actually came for: shipping.
